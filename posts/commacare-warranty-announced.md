# comma.ai Launches commacare: Extended Warranty Coverage Comes to comma four

comma.ai has officially introduced [commacare](https://blog.comma.ai/commacare/), a new extended warranty offering for the comma four. The announcement marks the company’s first modern subscription-linked hardware protection program and adds another layer to the growing ecosystem around openpilot ownership.

The new program extends warranty coverage on the comma four to a total of **two years**, but there’s a catch: it’s only available through a qualifying **comma prime** or **prime lite** subscription.

For a company that has historically focused heavily on open-source software and enthusiast-driven development, the move signals a continued push toward a more polished consumer ownership experience.

## What Is commacare?

At its core, commacare is a warranty extension program for the comma four.

Every comma four already includes a standard **1-year limited hardware warranty**, but commacare extends that coverage by up to an additional year — giving owners as much as **24 months of total protection**.

According to the official [commacare announcement](https://blog.comma.ai/commacare/):

- **comma prime ($24/month)** now includes:
  - commacare
  - built-in LTE connectivity
  - one year of cloud storage

- **prime lite ($14/month)** now also includes:
  - commacare
  - no included cellular service
  - optional bring-your-own SIM support

Existing prime lite subscribers locked in at the old $10/month pricing will reportedly keep their legacy rate unless they cancel.

Additional subscription details are available on the official [comma connect page](https://comma.ai/connect#what-is-commacare).

## How Users Become Eligible

comma.ai says users must:

1. Purchase a comma four
2. Pair it with [comma connect](https://connect.comma.ai/)
3. Sign up for comma prime or prime lite within 30 days of delivery

There is also a temporary grace period for existing comma four owners. Devices delivered earlier can still become eligible if subscribed before **June 18, 2026**.

Coverage status appears directly inside comma connect with a dedicated commacare badge.

## What commacare Covers — And What It Doesn’t

The company emphasizes that commacare is **not accidental damage insurance**.

Coverage mirrors the standard warranty and applies only to hardware defects. It does **not** cover:

- Theft
- Loss
- Accidental damage
- User-caused repair damage

Importantly, canceling the associated subscription immediately ends commacare coverage, and it cannot later be resumed. Details regarding prime cancellation and subscription behavior are outlined on the official [comma support page](https://comma.ai/support).

That detail has become one of the most discussed parts of the announcement.

## Community Reaction: Mixed but Passionate

As expected for a major comma.ai policy change, reactions from the openpilot community were immediate and varied.

The largest discussion appeared on Reddit’s [r/Comma_ai thread covering the announcement](https://www.reddit.com/r/Comma_ai/comments/1ti1lkt/introducing_commacare_an_extended_warranty_for/).

### Positive Reactions

Some users welcomed the move as a sign that comma.ai is maturing operationally and taking hardware reliability more seriously.

A few buyers noted that the inclusion of LTE, cloud features, and warranty protection together makes the full prime subscription easier to justify.

Others appreciated that existing subscribers were grandfathered into older pricing.

### Concerns About Subscription Lock-In

The biggest criticism centered around the fact that commacare is **subscription-bound** rather than a standalone purchase.

Several users argued that:

- a one-time extended warranty option would be preferable
- the effective cost feels high over two years
- warranty protection should not disappear if cloud services are canceled

One Reddit commenter summarized the sentiment bluntly:

> “Offer a 3-year warranty for $99 at the point of sale and I'm in.”

Others questioned whether the short extension period implied concerns about long-term hardware durability.

### Hardware Reliability Discussion Reignited

The announcement also reopened long-running discussions around historical reliability of older comma hardware generations.

Some users shared stories of:
- failed screens
- camera issues
- dead-on-arrival devices
- post-warranty failures

However, comma.ai staff pushed back on the narrative that failures are common.

In the Reddit thread, comma.ai representative Adeeb Shihadeh stated that the comma four has been the company’s most reliable hardware yet, claiming:

- comma two: ~26% RMA rate
- comma three: ~11%
- comma 3X: ~5%
- comma four: ~0.7%

The company says only 47 out of more than 7,100 comma four units shipped have required RMAs so far.

If accurate, those numbers represent a dramatic reliability improvement over previous generations.

## Why This Matters for the openpilot Ecosystem

The launch of commacare is significant beyond just warranty coverage.

It represents another step in comma.ai’s gradual shift from:
- enthusiast hardware startup
toward:
- polished consumer platform provider

Historically, openpilot users accepted a degree of tinkering, DIY troubleshooting, and hardware risk as part of the experience. But with:
- improved hardware design
- better manufacturing consistency
- LTE-enabled cloud services
- subscription offerings
- streamlined onboarding

…the ecosystem is becoming increasingly accessible to mainstream drivers.

That could matter tremendously as the driver-assistance market becomes more competitive and new openpilot-adjacent hardware alternatives continue appearing.

## The Bigger Strategic Picture

There’s also a business reality behind the move.

Recurring subscription revenue provides:
- more predictable operational income
- better support sustainability
- easier forecasting for warranty liabilities
- infrastructure funding for cloud services and LTE

At the same time, tying warranty protection to subscriptions creates ongoing tension with the enthusiast audience that originally helped grow openpilot.

The balance between:
- consumer-friendly services
and
- enthusiast-friendly openness

continues to be one of the most fascinating aspects of the comma.ai ecosystem.

## Final Thoughts

commacare is ultimately a relatively simple product:
an extended warranty bundled into comma.ai subscriptions.

But the announcement says a lot about where comma.ai is heading.

The company appears increasingly focused on:
- reliability
- operational maturity
- mainstream usability
- recurring services

Whether the community embraces subscription-linked hardware protection long term remains to be seen, but one thing is clear:

comma.ai is no longer operating purely like a scrappy open-source hardware experiment.

It’s evolving into a full-fledged consumer platform.
