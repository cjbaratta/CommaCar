If you are shopping for a used car specifically to run **openpilot**, the best choice is not always the newest car or the car with the flashiest factory driver-assist system. The sweet spot is usually a car that is already well-supported, easy to find used, and equipped with the right factory safety package.

As of May 2026, comma’s official supported-cars list says openpilot supports **333 vehicles**, and comma describes a supported vehicle as one that “just works” when you install a comma device. That said, compatibility can depend on year, trim, market, and factory options, so always verify the exact year and package before buying.\
Source: [comma.ai Supported Cars](https://docs.comma.ai/CARS/)

Below are some of the best used openpilot-friendly cars to shop for in 2026, broken down by body type with a lower-budget and higher-budget pick for each.

## What to Look for Before Buying

Before getting into the picks, here are the rules I would use when shopping:

1. **Confirm the exact year and trim on comma’s supported-cars page.** Do not assume a similar year works.
2. **Prioritize full-speed adaptive cruise control.** Stop-and-go support makes a big difference in traffic.
3. **Avoid unsupported Toyota Security Gateway models.** comma’s docs specifically note that some newer Toyota models, including Toyota RAV4 Prime 2021+, Sienna 2021+, Venza 2021+, Highlander 2024+, Corolla Cross 2022+ US models, Camry 2025+, and others are not yet supported due to Toyota’s newer message authentication method.
4. **Budget for the comma device and harness.** A cheap car is not really cheap if you forget to include the hardware cost.
5. **Check the windshield and camera area.** Prior glass replacement, tint, or sensor/camera issues can complicate install and performance.

Source: [comma.ai Supported Cars](https://docs.comma.ai/CARS/)

# Compact Sedan / Hatchback

## Lower-Budget Pick: 2020–2022 Toyota Corolla

The **2020–2022 Toyota Corolla** is one of the easiest used openpilot recommendations because it is affordable, reliable, efficient, and officially listed as supported for all trims in those years. comma’s docs list the 2020–2022 Corolla as supported with openpilot, 0 mph ACC minimum, and 0 mph resume from stop, which is exactly what most buyers want for commuting.

Used prices are also still reasonable. Kelley Blue Book lists 2020 Corolla pricing starting around **$14,500** for a Corolla L sedan and around **$16,200** for an SE sedan, with higher trims costing more.

Sources:  

* [comma.ai Supported Cars](https://docs.comma.ai/CARS/)  
* [Kelley Blue Book: 2020 Toyota Corolla](https://www.kbb.com/toyota/corolla/2020/)

### Why it is a good pick

It is the budget-friendly, low-drama choice. It is easy to find, cheap to run, and small enough for city driving while still being comfortable enough for highway use.

### What to watch for

Make sure you are looking at a supported 2020–2022 Corolla, not a newer unsupported Corolla Cross US model. Also check for windshield replacements or front camera issues.

- - -

## Higher-Budget Pick: 2021–2022 Toyota Corolla Hybrid

The **Corolla Hybrid** gives you many of the same openpilot benefits as the standard Corolla, but with better fuel economy and a calmer commuter personality. comma lists the 2020–2022 Corolla Hybrid as supported, with 0 mph ACC minimum and 0 mph resume from stop.

Source: [comma.ai Supported Cars](https://docs.comma.ai/CARS/)

### Why it is a good pick

For someone who drives a lot, the Corolla Hybrid is arguably one of the best value-per-mile openpilot cars. It is efficient, practical, and likely to be cheaper to own than many larger vehicles.

### What to watch for

Used hybrid pricing can stay stubbornly high. Compare it against a Camry Hybrid or Prius before buying, because sometimes the larger car is not much more expensive.

# Midsize Sedan

## Lower-Budget Pick: 2020–2023 Hyundai Sonata

The **2020–2023 Hyundai Sonata** is a strong openpilot pick if you want more comfort and space than a Corolla without jumping into luxury pricing. comma lists the 2020–2023 Sonata as supported with openpilot available, 0 mph ACC minimum, and 0 mph resume from stop. The Sonata Hybrid from 2020–2023 is also listed.

Source: [comma.ai Supported Cars](https://docs.comma.ai/CARS/)

### Why it is a good pick

The Sonata tends to depreciate more than Toyota and Honda alternatives, which can make it a good used value. It also has a comfortable cabin and a highway-friendly ride.

### What to watch for

Trim and package details matter. Confirm the exact car is supported and has the correct driver-assist hardware.

## Higher-Budget Pick: 2021–2024 Toyota Camry Hybrid

For a more proven long-term ownership choice, the **2021–2024 Toyota Camry Hybrid** is one of the best all-around openpilot-friendly sedans. comma lists the 2021–2024 Camry and Camry Hybrid as supported with openpilot, 0 mph ACC minimum, and 0 mph resume from stop.

Kelley Blue Book lists 2021 Camry pricing starting around **$19,050** for the LE sedan and around **$19,800** for the SE, with higher trims costing more.

Sources:  

* [comma.ai Supported Cars](https://docs.comma.ai/CARS/)  
* [Kelley Blue Book: 2021 Toyota Camry](https://www.kbb.com/toyota/camry/2021/)

### Why it is a good pick

It is comfortable, efficient in hybrid form, easy to service, and excellent for long highway drives.

### What to watch for

Avoid assuming the newest Camry is better for openpilot. comma’s docs specifically list the **2025+ Camry** under Toyota models not yet supported due to Toyota’s newer security approach.

# Pickup / Utility

## Lower-Budget Pick: 2022–2024 Ford Maverick

The **Ford Maverick** is one of the most interesting openpilot-compatible used vehicles because it gives you truck utility without full-size truck cost or size. comma lists the 2022 Maverick LARIAT Luxury and 2023–2024 Maverick with Co-Pilot360 Assist as supported with openpilot, 0 mph ACC minimum, and 0 mph resume from stop.

Source: [comma.ai Supported Cars](https://docs.comma.ai/CARS/)

It is compact, practical, fuel-efficient for a pickup, and useful for home projects or outdoor gear without feeling huge in daily driving.

The package matters a lot. For 2022, comma specifically calls out **LARIAT Luxury**. For 2023–2024, look for **Co-Pilot360 Assist**.

The **Maverick Hybrid** is the one I would personally prioritize if you want a small truck and can find one at a fair price. comma lists the 2022 Maverick Hybrid LARIAT Luxury and 2023–2024 Maverick Hybrid with Co-Pilot360 Assist as supported.

# EV Pick

## Best Lower-Budget EV: 2022–2023 Chevrolet Bolt EUV Premier

For an affordable EV with openpilot support, the **2022–2023 Chevy Bolt EUV Premier** is the standout. comma lists the 2022–2023 Bolt EUV Premier or Premier Redline, without the Super Cruise package, as supported with openpilot available.

Kelley Blue Book private-party values for a 2022 Bolt EUV Premier show roughly **$15,720 to $17,920** depending on condition, while current KBB used listings show many dealer examples around the high teens to low $20,000s.

Sources:  

* [comma.ai Supported Cars](https://docs.comma.ai/CARS/)  
* [Kelley Blue Book: 2022 Chevrolet Bolt EUV Premier](https://www.kbb.com/chevrolet/bolt-euv/2022/premier-hatchback-4d/)

### Why it is a good pick

It is inexpensive for an EV, practical, and efficient. If your driving is mostly local or regional, this may be the best value EV/openpilot combination.

### What to watch for

Avoid the Super Cruise package for this pick, because comma’s supported listing specifically says Bolt EUV Premier **without Super Cruise Package**. Also check battery recall history and battery health.

## Best Higher-Budget EV: 2022–2024 Hyundai Ioniq 5

If you want the EV that feels more modern and road-trip capable, the **Hyundai Ioniq 5** is the better higher-budget pick. comma lists the 2022–2024 Ioniq 5 with HDA II and without HDA II as supported with openpilot available, 0 mph ACC minimum, and 0 mph resume from stop.

Kelley Blue Book listings for lightly used 2026 Ioniq 5 SEL examples show prices in the low-to-mid $30,000s, while older 2022–2024 used examples will generally be the more relevant openpilot-shopping target.

Sources:  

* [comma.ai Supported Cars](https://docs.comma.ai/CARS/)  
* [Kelley Blue Book: Used Hyundai Ioniq 5 Listings](https://www.kbb.com/cars-for-sale/used/2026/hyundai/ioniq-5/sel)

### Why it is a good pick

It has fast charging, a roomy cabin, distinctive styling, and strong openpilot compatibility. For someone who wants an EV that works well as both a daily driver and road-trip car, the Ioniq 5 is the premium pick here.

### What to watch for

Confirm whether the car has HDA II or not, because comma lists different harness/connectors for Ioniq 5 with and without HDA II.

* **Best cheap commuter:** 2020–2022 Toyota Corolla

* **Best compact SUV:** 2019–2021 Toyota RAV4 Hybrid
* **Best newer SUV:** 2023–2026 Honda CR-V Hybrid
* **Best family hauler:** 2021–2026 Honda Odyssey
* **Best small truck:** 2022–2024 Ford Maverick Hybrid
* **Best budget EV:** 2022–2023 Chevy Bolt EUV Premier

For most buyers, the safest default choices are the **Toyota Corolla**, **Toyota Camry Hybrid**, **Toyota RAV4 Hybrid**, and **Hyundai Ioniq 5**. They combine strong availability, practical ownership costs, and official openpilot support.

The biggest advice: **do not buy based on model name alone.** openpilot compatibility is specific. Always check the exact year, trim, package, and connector requirements before money changes hands.
